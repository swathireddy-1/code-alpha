PHISHING AWARENESS TRAINING
===========================

PURPOSE
Learn how to recognize phishing emails, fake websites, and social engineering.
This material is for defensive awareness and safe online behavior.

1. WHAT IS PHISHING?
Phishing is an attempt to trick someone into revealing sensitive information,
opening an unsafe attachment, or taking an unsafe action. It may arrive by
email, text message, phone call, social media, or workplace messaging apps.

2. SPOTTING PHISHING EMAILS
Watch for:
- Unexpected requests for passwords, one-time codes, payments, or personal data.
- Urgent or threatening language.
- A sender name that looks familiar but uses an unfamiliar email address.
- Unexpected attachments or links.
- Generic greetings, unusual wording, or details that do not fit the situation.

3. CHECKING LINKS AND WEBSITES
- Read the full domain carefully and look for misspellings or extra words.
- Be cautious with shortened links and unexpected links.
- Do not enter credentials after following an unexpected message link.
- Visit the official website or app independently.
- A padlock / HTTPS means the connection is encrypted; it does not prove the
  website is genuine.

4. SOCIAL ENGINEERING
Social engineering manipulates people by using:
- Authority: pretending to be a manager or IT support.
- Urgency: pressuring someone to act without checking.
- Familiarity: pretending to be a colleague or trusted contact.
- Curiosity or rewards: tempting someone to open a file or claim a prize.
- Fear: threatening account closure or other consequences.

5. SAFE RESPONSE: STOP - CHECK - REPORT
STOP: Do not click, download, pay, reply, or share credentials.
CHECK: Verify through a separate trusted channel, such as a known phone number
       or the organization's approved support contact.
REPORT: Use the organization's phishing-report option or contact its IT/security
        team.

6. IF YOU ALREADY CLICKED OR SHARED INFORMATION
- Report it immediately and follow the organization's incident process.
- Change an exposed password through the official website or app.
- Change the password on other services if it was reused.
- Tell the security team if you shared a one-time code or approved an unexpected
  sign-in prompt.
- Contact your bank or provider through official contact details if payment
  information was involved.

7. GOOD SECURITY HABITS
- Use unique passwords and a trusted password manager.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Never share passwords or one-time codes.
- Keep devices, browsers, and security software updated.
- Confirm unusual requests using a second trusted channel.

REMEMBER
Think before you click. Pause, verify, and report.

This is general awareness material. Follow your workplace or school's own
security policies and reporting procedures.
